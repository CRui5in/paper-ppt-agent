# Writing

Apply these rules to manuscripts, speaker notes, and user-facing reports in the requested language.

- Choose the opening and structure from the material: explain a concept, work through a derivation, describe a method, compare evidence, or present a result. Keep planning labels separate from the visible text.
- Preserve facts, attribution, terminology, comparisons, negation, conditions, units, precision, and uncertainty. These take priority over style preferences.
- State applicability conditions directly. Include a limitation where it changes the conclusion, evidence interpretation, or correct use of the result; retain it beside a claim when that claim depends on it.
- Keep demonstrated problems, questions needing verification, and optional further work distinct in research notes. Carry into slide copy the findings and qualifications that affect the audience's understanding.
- Remove repeated disclaimers, apology-like framing, vague self-criticism, and explanations aimed only at hypothetical objections. In Chinese, inspect phrases such as “仅仅做了初步尝试”, “只能提供有限参考”, and “仍有很大提升空间”; replace vague self-assessment with the actual work, result, or condition.
- Use “可能”, “提示”, “初步”, “may”, and “suggests” when the evidence warrants them. Keep tradeoffs concrete: state the conditions, improved metric, and weaker metric; explain a cause only when supported.
- Treat stock phrases and sentence patterns as editing clues. Keep useful contrasts, negation, passive voice, connecting words, and lists with distinct items. Choose sentence length and punctuation for clarity.
- Use the source's level of detail. Keep definitions, theorems, and necessary quotations accurate and attributed. Revise only passages with an identifiable problem.
- Before delivery, compare the draft with its sources for additions, omissions, stronger claims, and changed conditions. Keep formatting tokens, figure IDs, source comments, and file contracts intact.
